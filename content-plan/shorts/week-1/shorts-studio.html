Your smartphone GPS still relies on a sailing map drawn in 1569. There is a genius reason why! In 2005, when Google built Google Maps, they picked the ancient Web Mercator projection. Why? Because when you navigate a city street on your phone, preserving ninety-degree street corners is everything. On other maps, square city blocks appear skewed into angled parallelograms. Mercator also flattens the inhabited world into a perfect square, which web servers easily chop into 256-pixel square tiles that load instantly across millions of smartphones. So the map isn't broken—it was perfectly engineered for digital navigation. Did you know your GPS was using a 450-year-old sailing trick? Drop your thoughts below!
