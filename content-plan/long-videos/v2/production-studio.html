12-Beat Long-Form Master Script (V2)

1,720 Words · Target Delivery: 12:48 min · 135 wpm

Script Locked
Beat 01 · The Scale Hook 0:00 – 0:45 · 110 words

Four entire European countries—the United Kingdom, France, Germany, and Italy—could fit inside this territory. Yet the total population of this vast land is smaller than the single city of Madrid. Mongolia spans one point five six million square kilometres of rolling steppe, snow-capped mountains, and the great Gobi desert, but across this entire expanse live just three point four million people. That produces a density of barely two people per square kilometre—the lowest of any sovereign nation on Earth. But the real story isn't just that Mongolia is empty. It is that half of the entire country's population now lives crammed inside a single valley.

Beat 02 · The Physical Geography 0:45 – 1:50 · 145 words

To understand why Mongolia never developed dense rural populations, you have to look at its extreme continental geography. Trapped between the Siberian taiga to the north and the arid expanse of China to the south, Mongolia sits at an average altitude of fifteen hundred metres above sea level. It has no coastline and receives virtually no maritime moisture. Less than one percent of its entire landmass is arable for growing crops. For three thousand years, human survival here was only possible through pastoral nomadism—moving herds of horses, sheep, goats, and camels across hundreds of miles of seasonal pastures. But in the 21st century, that nomadic cycle is breaking.

Beat 03 · The Dzud Engine 1:50 – 3:10 · 170 words

The destructive force driving this transformation is called the Dzud. A Dzud occurs when an intensely dry summer withers pasture grass, followed immediately by an catastrophic winter where temperatures plunge below minus forty-five degrees Celsius. Deep sheets of ice freeze over the ground, making it impossible for livestock to dig through the crust to feed. In severe Dzud years, millions of animals perish within a matter of weeks. When a herding family loses their entire livestock in a single winter, their nomadic way of life is instantly extinguished. With no animals left to herd, they pack their traditional round felt tent—a ger—onto a truck and head toward the only place with hospitals, electricity, and jobs: Ulaanbaatar.
