Every Map You've Seen Is Lying About Africa

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): Why Africa Is Bigger Than You Think
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "On the map on your wall, Greenland looks about the size of Africa. Africa is fourteen times bigger...."
The Central Question: If the map is this wrong, why do we still use it?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): Mercator is a conformal projection: it preserves angles, so a constant compass bearing is a straight line. Built in 1569 for navigation. Web maps still use a variant (Web Mercator) because conformality keeps shapes correct at every zoom level while panning. The trade-off is area — distortion grows with distance from the equator. Africa straddles the equator; Greenland sits near the pole. The map is not broken; it is optimised for a job you are not doing.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): Everyone shows the distortion. Almost nobody explains why we chose it and why we deliberately keep it. Our video argues the map is optimised, not broken.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: None · Sources: CIA World Factbook (areas); USGS map projections publications; EPSG/OGC Web Mercator spec; Natural Earth (basemap)

V2 Week 2 · Month 1 Day 14 Topic Score: 18 / 20

This Country Has More Empty Land Than People

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): The Country Where Almost Nobody Lives
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "Mongolia is larger than Britain, France, Germany and Italy combined. Its entire population would not fill one mid-sized city...."
The Central Question: Why is a country this large this empty — and why is it emptying further?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): Continental interior plus the rain shadow of the Himalaya and Tibetan Plateau means very low precipitation. Steppe supports pastoral herding at extremely low carrying capacity, not settled agriculture. Then the modern layer: severe winter events (dzud) destroy livestock, herders lose their livelihood and move to Ulaanbaatar. So the emptiness is not static — it is accelerating into one city.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): Most videos stop at 'it is cold and empty.' Ours follows the movement — dzud to Ulaanbaatar — so the emptiness is a process, not a fact.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: None · Sources: National Statistics Office of Mongolia; World Bank density series; UN World Population Prospects; FAO dzud reporting

V3 Week 3 · Month 1 Day 21 Topic Score: 17 / 20

The Town Where Your Kitchen Is in Another Country

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): The Border That Runs Through People's Houses
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "In this town the border runs through front doors, living rooms, and at least one cafe. There are more than twenty pieces of one country sitting inside the other...."
The Central Question: How does a border this absurd survive into the 21st century?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): Medieval land transactions, not wars. Plots were swapped between the Lord of Breda and the Duke of Brabant from 1198 onward, recorded plot-by-plot rather than as a line. When Belgium and the Netherlands separated in 1830 and fixed the boundary in 1843, the treaty froze plot-level ownership instead of drawing a clean line. The last parcels were only settled in 1995. The border is what happens when property law outlives the states that wrote it.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): Nearly all coverage treats it as a quirky curiosity. Ours treats it as a records story: a border made of property deeds, not politics.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: None — settled in 1995, both EU members · Sources: 1843 Treaty of Maastricht (boundary); Baarle-Hertog and Baarle-Nassau municipal records; Dutch Kadaster

V4 Week 4 · Month 1 Day 28 Topic Score: 19 / 20

The River That Runs Out Before It Reaches the Sea

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): 40 Million People Depend on a River That No Longer Reaches the Sea
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "Around forty million people depend on this river. In most years since the 1960s, it has not reached the sea...."
The Central Question: How do you use a river until it stops existing?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): The 1922 Colorado River Compact divided the water using flow measured during the wettest stretch on record. Roughly 16.5 million acre-feet were allocated against a long-run average well below that. The river was over-allocated on paper before a major dam existed. Reservoir decline and the structural deficit are the arithmetic arriving, decades late. This is not primarily a drought story.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): News frames it as drought. Ours frames it as an arithmetic error made in 1922 and never corrected.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: Low — interstate and legal, not international. Do not frame any US state or Mexico as at fault. · Sources: Colorado River Compact 1922; US Bureau of Reclamation 24-Month Study; USGS streamflow records; Minute 319/323

V5 Week 5 · Month 2 Day 35 Topic Score: 18 / 20

What Happens When a Canal Runs Out of Water

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): The Canal That Runs on Rain, Not Seawater
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "The Panama Canal does not run on seawater. It runs on rain — and every ship that crosses uses roughly fifty million gallons of fresh water...."
The Central Question: What happens to global trade when one lake gets low?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): It is a lock canal sitting about 26 metres above sea level, fed by Gatun Lake — rainfall-fed, and also the drinking-water source for a large share of Panama's population. Every transit spills fresh water to the sea. When lake levels fall, the canal authority cuts daily transit slots and draft limits, which creates queues, auction prices for slots, and rerouting via Suez or the Cape. A 1914 design trade-off sends its bill when rainfall shifts.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): Coverage says 'drought slows canal.' Ours explains that it is a freshwater lock canal by 1914 design, and that design is the actual cause.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: Low — describe canal operations factually, no commentary on any government · Sources: Panama Canal Authority (ACP) transit statistics and draft advisories; UNCTAD Review of Maritime Transport; IMF PortWatch

V6 Week 6 · Month 2 Day 42 Topic Score: 17 / 20

The Island 1,700 Miles From the Nearest Town

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): How 240 People Run a Country in the Middle of the Atlantic
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "There is no airport. The only way in is a boat that takes about six days, and it goes a handful of times a year...."
The Central Question: How does a place this isolated pay for itself?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): The economy is essentially licensing. A rock-lobster fishery concession, stamps and coins sold to collectors, and fishing-rights revenue tied to one of the Atlantic's largest marine protected areas. Land is communally held and outsiders cannot buy in. Isolation is not romantic — it is an economic model with a very narrow base, and every part of it is a deliberate choice to stay viable.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): Everyone films the remoteness. Almost nobody opens the balance sheet and shows how isolation is financed.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: Describe territory status factually only. No sovereignty framing. · Sources: Tristan da Cunha Government; UK FCDO territory pages; Blue Belt Programme / MPA documentation

V7 Week 7 · Month 2 Day 49 Topic Score: 17 / 20

The Countries That Will Lose Half Their People

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): What Happens to a Country When Everyone Leaves
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "Some countries are projected to have fewer than half as many people in 2100 as they do today. None of them are at war...."
The Central Question: What does depopulation do to the map itself?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): Fertility below replacement plus out-migration, compounding over decades. The geographic consequence is what matters: interior and rural regions empty first, because services follow density. The school closes, then the clinic, then road maintenance stops — and the remaining people leave. Coasts and capitals hold. The country physically contracts around a few nodes while its borders stay exactly the same.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): Demography channels give the numbers. Ours gives the geography — where the emptying happens and why the interior goes first.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: CAUTION — avoid all migration-politics and nationalist framing. Pure services-density mechanism. Label every projection QUALIFIED. · Sources: UN World Population Prospects 2024 (medium variant); Eurostat; relevant national statistics offices

V8 Week 8 · Month 2 Day 56 Topic Score: 18 / 20

Two Thirds of This Country Lives in One City

A/B Title Option (Alt Angle): The Country Whose Interior Nobody Can Live In
First 5 Seconds Hook & Opening Words: "Iceland is roughly the size of Kentucky. Around two in three Icelanders live in one metropolitan area — and it is not really by choice...."
The Central Question: Why does an entire country cling to one ring around the edge?
The Mechanism (The Real Answer): The interior highlands are volcanic desert — glaciers, lava fields and sand plains, with no arable land and no year-round road access. Settlement has always been a coastal ring, and the Ring Road is effectively the country's only spine. Modern concentration then compounds it: port, university, hospital and airport all sit in the capital region.
Differentiating Angle (Why We Stand Out): Iceland content is travel content. Ours is a geology-decides-settlement story with the Ring Road as the spine.
Neutrality & Fact-Check Guardrails: None · Sources: Statistics Iceland; Icelandic Meteorological Office; Vegagerdin (road administration); Iceland GeoSurvey
